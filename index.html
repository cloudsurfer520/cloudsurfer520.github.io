How This Tool Works

Overview

Checks whether a commuter flight can realistically get you from your departure airport to an Open Time trip's origin, with your own buffers built in. Times convert automatically between UTC and local using the IANA timezone database (DST-aware) — no manual math.

Quick Fill From OT Email Line

Optional. The OT email's trip table lists each operating leg — copy the first operating leg's row and paste it here (tab- or space-separated), e.g. 5Y734 SCL MIA Wed26Aug 0001z 0836z. It pulls the origin airport, day, and UTC departure time and fills the fields below automatically.

If that first leg departs from an airport with no commercial service (ZAZ, DNA, and similar cargo-only or military fields are common examples), set OT Origin to a nearby airport you can actually fly commercial into instead, and add the extra ground-transport ("limo") time onto Arrive Buf so the math still accounts for getting from that airport to the real OT origin.

Departure / OT Origin

Your gateway airport and the airport the Open Time trip departs from. Both use a built-in table of about 1,440 airports worldwide for timezone lookup. If an airport isn't recognized, a box appears to enter its IANA timezone manually (e.g. America/Chicago).

OT Trip Scheduled Departure — UTC

Day of month, then 24-hour UTC time, straight from the email (e.g. 28 / 1429). The day resolves to the nearest upcoming date — no need to think about month or year.

Searching As Of — UTC

Read-only reference clock the whole calculation is based on. Press Now to refresh it to the current time — this also refreshes the OT departure default and Flight Date box below, as long as you haven't typed your own values into them.

Depart / Arrive Buffers

Depart Buf: minimum hours after your search time before a commuter flight can realistically depart. Arrive Buf: minimum hours between landing at the OT origin and the OT trip's own departure — this is also where you'd add ground-transport time if you're flying into a nearby alternate airport instead of the OT origin itself (see Quick Fill above).

Feasibility Window

Once the OT trip details are filled in, this shows the latest local time you can arrive by, and how large your window is.

Open Google Flights Button

Opens Google Flights in a new tab, pre-filled with your route, date, one-way, and Business Class. The date used is whatever's in the Flight Date box below; if that's empty, it uses your earliest allowable departure date instead.

Flight Date — Local, At Origin

Day of month only. Defaults to today's date if it's currently 6pm local time at your departure airport or earlier, otherwise tomorrow's — auto-refreshed whenever you press Now, unless you've typed your own value.

Paste Copied Flight Results

Paste either a list of flights or a single flight's expanded details from Google Flights — the tool detects which one automatically:

  • A list produces a sortable feasibility table (direct flights first, then by journey time, tightest passing options on top). Tap any row to copy that flight's booking-request text to your clipboard.
  • An expanded flight's details produces a ready-to-send booking request instead, with flight numbers, dates, and times — no feasibility window needed for this one. This works the same whether it's a single nonstop or a multi-leg itinerary with connections; every leg gets chained into the request automatically.

Parsing runs automatically the moment you paste. The Go button is a manual fallback for anywhere that auto-paste detection doesn't fire.

A Note On Accuracy

This is a planning aid, not a booking system — always confirm the actual commuter flight's schedule directly with the airline before committing to bid on an Open Time trip.
